Awareness

Know the signs, act early

Colorectal disease is highly treatable when found early. These are the points the society most wants the public to know.

Start here

Screening from age 45

Most colorectal cancers grow from polyps that cause no symptoms at all. Screening finds and removes them before they turn, which is why it is offered from 45 — earlier if a close relative has been affected.

Read the national guidelines
  • 01

    Do not wait out bleeding

    Blood in the stool is common and usually benign — but it is never something to monitor on your own. Have it looked at.

  • 02

    Note lasting changes in habit

    A change in bowel pattern that persists beyond a few weeks is worth reporting, even when nothing else feels wrong.

  • 03

    Family history moves the date

    A parent or sibling with colorectal cancer usually means starting screening earlier. Tell your doctor about it.

  • 04

    Daily habits matter

    Fibre, movement, a healthy weight and avoiding tobacco all lower risk over a lifetime. None of them replace screening.

This section is general health awareness, not medical advice. For anything concerning you personally, please consult a qualified physician.
